Price caveats: Token-metered Seedance quotes are based on the provider’s reference frame area and are estimates. The actual square sample has a comparable pixel budget to a 16:9 480p clip, but final frame size, duration, discounts, and model parameters can change the invoice. fal Seedance 2.0’s 480p and 4K entries are computed from its published token formula; its other entries use the displayed rate. Atlas Wan Prime shows a native “4K” price in the pricing popup, but the image-to-video API schema only lists 4k-esr, so the uncallable native price is excluded here. “SR/ESR” is upscaled or enhanced output, not native detail.

Sizes shown in the samples

  • 480p · tested: four Atlas models and the earlier fal Seedance 2.5 clip. Watch the motion and lighting in each; the clips differ even at the same size.
  • 768p · tested: MiniMax H3’s dragon moves its head and gaze. Its extra pixels make sharpness harder to compare directly with the 480p clips.
  • 720p and 1080p · prices listed: these sizes appear in the provider rate cards, but we did not make matching sample clips at those sizes.

Beyond 1080p

  • 2K: H3 lists this tier on both providers. Atlas’s price matches fal’s $0.13/s; visual quality remains untested here.
  • 1440p: Atlas Mini, Seedance 2.0, 2.5, and Wan list SR/ESR outputs. They are enhanced images, not direct evidence of extra generated detail.
  • 4K: Atlas Seedance 2.0 and fal Seedance 2.0 list native 4K. Atlas Seedance 2.5 and Wan list 4K ESR; fal H3 lists 4K. None was rendered in this study. The 960×960 source cannot verify true 4K scene detail, so a future test should inspect texture, silhouette stability, temporal flicker, and the 4K invoice before marketing it as higher quality.
